# Taxonomy doctrine

How MCHPAI is permitted to name the things it observes. Naming is an act of
commitment; these rules make sure reality, not convenience, does the committing.

## Rule 1 — Existence precedes naming

Before asking *what* a population is, ask *whether* it is. A cluster is a
hypothesis until it is shown to exist independently of the method that found it.
We measure existence (bimodality of a defining axis, stability under resampling)
before we ever reach for a label.

## Rule 2 — Replication precedes taxonomy

Internal stability (bootstrap, consensus) is **necessary but not sufficient**. It
shows `P(cluster | this dataset)` is robust — not `P(cluster | reality)`. A
structure becomes taxonomy only when it recurs in **fresh, independent samples**
under **different conditions**. Clustering proposes; replication disposes.

## Rule 3 — Names are privileges earned through stability

`Cluster2` does not become `JupiterRouter` because a Gaussian mixture drew a
boundary. It earns a name only after its geometry, membership, profile and
behavior reproduce across fresh sessions. Until then it keeps its anonymous id.
Names, in MCHPAI, are discovered — never invented.

## Rule 4 — Dead clusters are victories, not failures

When a candidate dissolves under resampling — as `Cluster0` did — that is
**progress**. A hypothesis was killed by evidence. An observatory that cannot
kill its own hypotheses is not observing; it is confirming. We celebrate
dissolution.

## Rule 5 — Continuous spaces are preferred over artificial categories

Nature gives gradients, not islands. Soft separation (silhouette ≈ 0.2) is the
expected texture of behavior, not a defect. Where reality is continuous, the
honest representation is a position in space, and `distance(a, b)` may matter more
than `a.class == b.class`. We resist boxing a gradient. *(We have not built a
behavioral embedding: the evidence suggests it, but has not yet demanded it.
Rule 0 — we do not build what reality has not asked for.)*
